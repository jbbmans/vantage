# Design system

What ships. One stylesheet, `src/styles/index.css`, defines every token and shared component rule;
`tailwind.config.ts` maps the tokens to utilities. The public page (`src/pages/PublicSite.css`) and the
sign-in page (`src/styles/login-premium.css`) are the only other stylesheets, and both are scoped to
their own root. The six overlapping stylesheets this replaced (`brand-2026.css`,
`experience-motion.css`, `public-site.css`, `public-showcase.css`, `public-site-a11y.css`,
`login-reference.css`) are gone. Brand rules are in `docs/brand-2026.md`. (The root `DESIGN.md`
describes a different product's styling and is not the Vantage design system.)

## Dress blues (2026-11-17)

The look since 2026-11-17 is "dress blues": a formal, document-like frame. The rail, the top bar, each page's masthead
and the phone's tab bar stand on the coat's navy (`--frame`, the root's `--rail`) over a warm paper canvas
(`--canvas: 245 244 240`); navy ink on white cards (18:1). Gold (`--marker`) marks the page you are on, the eyebrow of a
masthead and a short rule over key figures. Scarlet (`--signal`, Cobalt only; the accent in every other palette) is kept
for action and attention: Log activity, the phone's centre button and unread badges. Every other palette keeps its own
hue family. What stands on the navy takes the dark tokens (`.on-navy`), so its text and controls keep their contrast.

The **masthead** is a page's header (`PageHeader`) on the navy ground: the eyebrow in gold with a short rule, the title in
the book serif, the lede and the page's actions, and along its foot the **piping**, a gold pinline over a scarlet stripe.
The piping appears once per page and nowhere else. In the consoles the masthead is a plate inside the page. Print sends
the navy back to ink on paper.

A counseling's worksheet is paper, not the app, and keeps paper's colours and Arial/Courier in every theme (ADR-0033).

The colour setting (Settings → Appearance) picks a whole palette, not only the signal colour. There are
twelve: Cobalt (the brand, above), Ocean, Teal, Forest, Olive, Coyote, Desert, Ember, Scarlet & Gold,
Rose, Violet and Steel. Each sets its own `--accent` family; `--marker`, the rail's active marker and
its glows (gold on Cobalt and on Scarlet & Gold); and a family of neutrals in its hue (canvas,
surfaces, lines, ink, rail, deep, and the shadow tint `--shadow-rgb`), in light and dark.

The palettes are generated by `scripts/palettes.mjs` (`--write` rewrites the block in `index.css`). It
carries Cobalt's neutrals to each hue in OKLCH, role for role, and refuses to write a palette that
fails a contrast pair: ink on cards 14:1 or better, muted text 4.5:1 or better on every fill, the signal
colour 4.5:1 as text and under its own ink, the marker 3:1 on the rail. Nothing in a component may name
a colour: tooltips, icon tiles, scrims, the marker and the glows read these tokens, so a palette reaches
every pixel of the app. A server test checks the setting's list and the stylesheet's palettes agree.
The public site keeps the brand palette.

Every muted tone clears WCAG AA on the darkest surface it can land on. Axe checks both themes on every
core page, the case page, the Reference and the public page (`tests/browser/06-a11y.spec.ts`,
`22-demo.spec.ts`), and every WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 A and AA rule, at any impact, at 320, 390, 768 and 1280 px
(`55-a11y-widths.spec.ts`). The light `--warn` is `138 79 5`, 5.2:1 or better as text and on its badge.

## Depth

Shadows are tinted navy, never black, and come as tokens: `--shadow-hairline` (a 1px ring instead of a
grey border), `--shadow-card`, `--shadow-lift`, `--shadow-pop` (menus, popovers) and `--shadow-modal`.
All of them read `--shadow-rgb`, the palette's ink. Dark mode swaps them for deeper, black-based versions
with a faint light ring. `--highlight` is the
inset top light on raised controls.

## Type

Geist (variable, served from `public/fonts`, OFL) for everything but page titles, with Inter as the fallback while it
loads. Page titles (every `h1`) are set in a book serif already on the machine, at regular weight: Iowan Old Style on a
Mac, Charter on Linux, Sitka or Cambria on Windows, Georgia last. No remote fonts. Figures use tabular numerals (`.fig`, `.stat-value`). Headlines are tight
(negative tracking), sentence case, semibold. Eyebrows are small uppercase labels, preceded by a short
accent rule in page headers. JetBrains Mono appears only in code and identifiers that need it.

## Shape and motion

Corners are square-cut, like a well-made form: 3 / 4 / 4 / 5 / 6 / 8 / 10px (`rounded-sm` … `rounded-3xl`); a card is
6px, a control 4px, a chip 3px; `rounded-full` stays for dots and avatars. Cards have a hairline edge rather than a
soft shadow, and buttons are flat with a darker underside. Inner elements use a smaller radius than their container.

Motion uses one curve, `--ease-spring: cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)`, and animates transform and opacity
only. Pages rise in (`page-in`), sections stagger (`section-in`), menus pop (`pop-in`), drawers slide.
`prefers-reduced-motion` turns all of it off. On the public page, reveal motion moves elements but
never hides them: at rest everything is opaque (asserted by `20-public-site.spec.ts`).

## Shell

A navy rail (collapsible with `[`) holding the workspace chip, grouped destinations with their `G`
shortcuts shown on hover, and the account. A translucent sticky header with the search pill (⌘K opens
the command palette, which also searches the Reference) and the "Log activity" pill. One demo banner in
the synthetic demo, with a segmented persona switch. An update banner appears when a newer build is
published (the service worker and `/api/health` both carry the build's hash).

The rail's groups are the information architecture's (`src/config/nav.ts`, ADR-0016): Today, Work, Records,
Personnel, Training & Development, Reports & Analytics and Administration, with Help and Settings at the foot. A group
opens to its pages; a long label wraps to a second line rather than being cut short, and a link to a console carries
an outward arrow, in the rail and in the strip of a group's pages. One link is marked as the page you are on
(`aria-current="page"`); a collapsed rail's group icon that opens another page is marked as the group you are in
(`aria-current="true"`), and both look the same.

Below the large breakpoint a **tab bar** (`src/components/TabBar.tsx`) holds Today, Work, a raised log button,
Records (Personnel for someone who leads a team) and More (the drawer). It is solid, so nothing scrolls through behind its labels, marks the current destination with the
accent and a short top marker, and hides while a field has focus or the drawer is open. While it shows, `--tabbar`
on the root is its height, so toasts and the footer sit above it.

## Components

`src/components/ui/primitives.tsx`: Button (one primary per screen), Input, NumberInput, Textarea,
Field (label, hint and error wired to the control), Select (Radix), Badge, Panel, EmptyState, Stat,
Segmented, Tabs, Progress, PageHeader, Switch, Tooltip. `Dialog`/`ConfirmDialog`, `toast` and `Menu`
are restyled on the same tokens.

What every one of them keeps (ADR-0032, `tests/browser/56-keyboard.spec.ts`):
- **Focus** is a 2 px accent outline (`:root :focus-visible`), never a shadow, so no component's own shadow hides it.
  `keepFocusClear` (`src/lib/keyboard.ts`) scrolls a keyboard-focused control out from under the sticky header and the
  tab bar.
- **A `Table` has a `label`**, its caption (hidden unless `showCaption`). While it is wider than its box its scroller is
  a named region that takes a Tab stop (`ScrollRegion`, `useOverflow`).
- **A `Dialog`** gives focus back to what opened it, even when its owner unmounts it; a body longer than the dialog takes
  a Tab stop.
- **`Tabs`** take a `label` and the id of their `TabPanel`, and move with the arrow keys, Home and End.
- **A busy `Button`** stays focusable (`aria-disabled`, `aria-busy`) and ignores presses.
- **A refused save** puts each message on its `Field` and focus on the first (`useRefusal`, `missing()`).
- **Single-key shortcuts** obey the person's `shortcuts` preference (`shortcutsOn`).

Case components (`src/components/work.tsx`): `StageBadge` (colours from `STAGE_TONE`; waiting shows its
category) and `WorkRow` (document number, stage, overdue flag, next step or waiting time, due date).

FMRA components (`src/components/fmra/`):
- `LifecycleBars`: commitment, obligation, delivered and paid on one scale. The open residual is a
  hatched segment labelled with its condition (OCMT, UDOU, DOU, OTO), so the gap never rests on colour.
  A figure the source did not show is a dashed outline marked "Not shown", never a zero. It carries a
  screen-reader table; `decorative` draws an unlabelled illustration for empty states.
- `DiagnosisView`: the eight-part reading in the order the reference teaches.
- `Diagnoser`: figures in, reading out, and "open a case" with the figures already recorded.

`QueryFailure` (`src/components/QueryFailure.tsx`) tells offline, signed out, denied, missing and
server error apart, with a retry where one can help.

## Charts (ADR-0028)

The analytics kit (`src/components/analytics/`) draws every chart outside the FMRA components. No chart library.

- **The heading is the question** the chart answers ("How long have the open cases been open?"), with one sentence
  under it that states the answer, so the chart is never the only way to read it. Where the question is about some
  series ("Which requirements are falling behind?"), the sentence names the bar with the most of them; where each bar is
  a level on its day (errors open on a report), it gives the latest and how it moved, and never adds them up.
- **Every framed chart has its table.** The Chart/Table switch (tabs with their panels) shows the same figures in a table
  captioned by the question, its first column named for what a bar is ("Open for", "Requirement"); a print carries both
  and drops the switch. A short list of bars in a panel (the home dashboard's "Who holds what") has no switch: each bar
  is already its label, value and detail in words.
- **A counting mark opens its records where there are records to list.** A bar, column, tile or table cell that counts
  records is a button named "label: value. Show the N records", reachable by keyboard and with a visible focus ring; the
  dialog lists the records with links, says when it shows the first 200, and checks that they add up to the figure. A
  figure with nothing to list to this reader (an error report's series, the training standing without member detail, the
  home dashboard's holders) is an image named the same way, with no dead button. Paired columns have no "All": opened
  and closed added together count no record.
- **Forms.** `KpiTiles` for headline figures; `BarChart` for a distribution, a standing or an age band (stacked when a
  bar is parts of a whole); `ColumnChart` for a figure over time (paired when two series share a unit, such as opened and
  closed); `RateMeter` for a share. On the Unit dashboard, the analysis and in the reports named in ADR-0028, months,
  weeks and reports are columns; categories are bars.
- **Colour.** One series is `--accent`; a second is `--ink-3`. Status tones (`--good`, `--warn`, `--bad`) only for a
  series that is a status (met, due soon, past due); "no record" is `--ink-3` hatched, so it stands out from the empty
  track. A legend names every series, and every value is also in words, so nothing rests on colour.
- **Values on marks** only when they fit (up to 8 columns, 4 paired); the axis labels every few months; the table holds
  the rest.
- **Reports** draw the same forms on paper: bars across, or columns for a figure over time, each ending "From the table
  X, column Y."

## Patterns

- **Cards** summarize something that opens: every figure links to the records behind it.
- **Tables** compare records: the queue, workload by person, calculation inputs.
- **Charts** only where shape matters, and each answers a question (see Charts below): `LifecycleBars`
  for a case's lifecycle, the analytics kit for everything else.
- **Gates are shown before they refuse.** A step waiting on evidence (a verification, a passed funds
  check) says so in a warning strip with a link to the step that satisfies it, and its button is
  disabled; the server enforces the same rule.
- **Corrections are added, never edited.** An observation's "Correct" action supersedes it; the
  original stays in the history marked "Corrected later", and any calculation that used it shows as
  stale.
- **Disclosure** holds secondary detail: "How to do this", "Everything the source said", procedure
  source and limitations, the causes the reference offers. Routine actions are never hidden behind one.
  Secondary filters fold behind a "Filters" button that counts the ones set and opens when a link sets one
  (Activities); search and the period stay in view.
- **Tutorials** (ADR-0034). A page whose task has a tutorial names it in one line under its title (`TutorialPrompt`);
  nothing opens by itself. The tutorial opens in a panel at the foot of the screen, not a dialog, so the page stays
  usable: every step listed, the open one with **Go to** (another page) or **Show me** (outlines the control for a few
  seconds and focuses it). It folds to its heading on a phone and wherever it would cover the control, and says it
  waits while a modal window is open. A step points at a control on the page, never inside a form; a control a step
  names carries a static `data-tutorial` name, and its visible label is the step's `control`. Words follow the
  house rules: at most two plain sentences a step, the control's own name, no promise the server does not keep.
- **Page headers.** The heading is the page's menu label, or that label made specific to the person ("Your
  record", "JEPES readiness"); the eyebrow is its group, from `usePlace()`, with the unit on a unit's pages. A
  sentence that names a place uses `placeName()` ("Records → Awards"), never a hand-written path.
- **Action names.** The same action has the same name everywhere:

  | Action | Name |
  |---|---|
  | Quick capture (N), from anywhere | **Log activity** |
  | Create a record of a kind | **New …**: New activity, New award, New training, New counseling, New goal, New task, New report; the dialog it opens has the same title, and the page's empty state offers the same button |
  | Bring data in | **Import …** naming what comes in: Import activities, Import work |
  | Take data out | **Export …** naming what goes out: Export activities, Export analysis PDF, Export JEPES input PDF |

  Import and export are also in ⌘K. A button never says only the format ("CSV", "PDF").
- **One demo indicator.** Pages show no other demo labels, except the flagship item's synthetic system
  values, labelled where they appear.
- **Language.** Real names for real concepts: document, tasker, stage, waiting on posting, handed off,
  verified, not shown. No table names or adapter names in the interface.

## Public page

Its own fixed palette (it has no stored preference to follow): deep navy hero and footer around a cool
paper body, brand teal as the accent on dark and cobalt on light. A floating glass navigation pill,
double-bezel frames around product stills (drawn in HTML, so they are crisp and readable to crawlers),
an asymmetric bento, pill calls to action with a nested icon. It is prerendered to static HTML at build
time and must stay whole without JavaScript.

## Not used

Streaks, gamification, tactical styling, purple gradients, AI branding, decorative KPI walls,
arbitrary animation, grey drop shadows, 1px grey borders on cards.
