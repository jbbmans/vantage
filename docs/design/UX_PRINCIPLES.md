# UX principles

1. **Answer the screen's question first.** Today puts "your work" and "waiting" above everything else.
   A leader's Today puts unassigned, overdue, blocked and waiting work first.
2. **One next step, not a wizard.** A procedure case shows the current step's form. Every other step is
   one click away in the checklist, and "Add a finding / note / question / value" is always there for
   anything that does not fit.
3. **Type it once.** Values from the sheet enter the case as source-file observations. Calculations read
   recorded values. Drafts assemble recorded facts. Quick capture parses a sentence.
4. **Say where every number came from.** Each value carries its source label. A calculation cites each
   input. A count carries its definition and window.
5. **Distinguish what is easy to confuse.** Prepared vs submitted, approved vs posted, waiting vs working,
   candidate vs decision, holding work vs credit for it.
6. **Never label people.** Counts sit beside what someone holds, what they are waiting on, and the
   stated limits of what was captured.
7. **Private means private.** Drafts and career plans say so where they are written.
8. **Recover from conflict plainly.** A stale write returns "This work changed while you were looking at
   it. Reload to see what changed, then try again." The page reloads its data after every write,
   successful or not.
9. **Reload, back and deep links always work.** Work items and every tab have their own URL.
10. **Accessible by default.** Labels on every control, one `h1` per page, real buttons for tooltips,
    keyboard reachable, AA contrast in both themes, no sideways scrolling at 390px.
